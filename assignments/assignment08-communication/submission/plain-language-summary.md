# Plain-Language Summary

**Audience:** Public health analysts reviewing BMI patterns across income groups.

Income is one of several factors that may be connected to a person's health. Looking at average BMI across different income groups can help us recognize patterns and understand which questions may be worth exploring further.

The corrected visualization from Assignment 5 compares average BMI among adults aged 20–80 across three income groups and the available NHANES survey periods. The graph shows that average BMI differs between income groups, although these differences are not necessarily consistent across every survey period.

While these patterns are useful to examine, the graph does not explain why they occur. Factors such as access to healthcare, living conditions, and lifestyle may also contribute to differences in BMI, but they were not examined in this analysis.

The results come from a prepared NHANES classroom dataset and are descriptive and unweighted. Records with missing information were excluded, along with groups containing fewer than 30 observations. For these reasons, the findings cannot be generalized to the US population or used to conclude that income causes changes in BMI.

Further research using appropriate survey weights and additional health-related factors could help provide a clearer understanding of these patterns.
